# Sayings Review: the best of the 100, aligned to the Realm

Status: recommendation, October 31, 2026. Nothing here is trademark-cleared (see the rules at the end). The catalog in [concept-catalog.md](concept-catalog.md) keeps its original Launch / Develop / Hold statuses because the generator reads them; this document is the Realm-aligned ranking that supersedes them for planning.

## Why the catalog needed a second pass

- 75 of 100 sayings are marked "Launch", so the status does not help anyone choose.
- The catalog was written before the Realm. Four of the live founding ten (Nos. 07–10) are not in it, and its own "first ten" still names a different 07–10.
- Several lines lean on formats already everywhere on the internet (the "and all I got was this" template, "could have been an email", "touch grass", "left the chat"), which makes them weaker as a brand and harder to protect.
- Some are too long for one chest print, or need a back print, which breaks the front-only rule.

## How each saying was judged

A saying makes the shortlist only if it passes all five tests:

1. **Standalone.** It lands as text on a shirt with no graphic explaining it.
2. **Short.** Eight words or fewer, readable at a glance, front only.
3. **Specific.** It describes a recognizable behaviour or system, not a generic complaint about the internet.
4. **Realm-fit.** It has a natural House, an office or title, and a form (Public Notice, Court & Order, or Royal Commemoration). Realm elements are optional, never required to understand the joke.
5. **Distinct.** It is not a stock meme template and does not point at a real person or company.

## Rules that still apply

- None of these sayings, "Low Value Content", "Royal House of Engagement" or "Much Published, Little Said" is cleared. Run a trademark search before art or inventory for any of them. Do not describe any of them as cleared.
- Every design stays front-only. A saying that needs a back print is cut or rewritten, not accommodated.
- No real people or companies on a garment. Historical facts are fine; names are not.
- Latin is subordinate texture, never the joke, and every Latin line is checked by a Latin reader and glossed in the Archive note on the product page.
