# Realm Garment System V2

> **Status: archived as a premium-edition exploration.** The launch system is now governed by [`launch-collection-matrix-v3.md`](launch-collection-matrix-v3.md) and the master manifest. All launch garments are front-only; sleeve seals, back prints, and garment QR codes remain optional future treatments after sample and wash testing.

## The problem this pass addresses

The original six designs are editorial, spare, contemporary, and saying-led. The newer Royal House work is ornate, heraldic, historical, and object-led. Both are strong, but simply placing them in one catalog does not make them one collection.

V2 treats each garment as a two-sided argument:

- **The front is the observation.** It must work immediately as a complete Low Value Content shirt.
- **The reverse is the institution.** The Royal House of Engagement supplies the Great House, office, reign, rank, and absurd provenance.
- **The sleeve is the bridge.** One compact House seal makes the two faces feel intentionally related.
- **The maker remains quiet.** Low Value Content appears as the source mark, not the dominant mythology.

## Visual hierarchy

1. **60–70% — The joke:** readable at conversational distance and understandable without scanning anything.
2. **20–30% — The Realm:** heraldry, office, title, lineage, or commemoration that rewards a closer look.
3. **5–10% — The maker and route:** a small Low Value Content mark and permanent QR destination.

This is a hierarchy of attention, not a literal percentage of printable area.

## Shared garment grammar

### Front

- Preserve the original sentence and its core typographic idea.
- Use one ceremonial device at most: a frame, rule, seal, small crown, or Latin line.
- Avoid turning every saying into a full coat of arms.

### Left sleeve

- One-color House seal, approximately 2–2.5 inches.
- Identifies allegiance without competing with the front.
- The six House marks must ultimately be redrawn as a coherent vector family.

### Upper back

- A small non-scannable preview seal in concept mockups.
- Production garments may use a tested permanent QR code only after size, contrast, curvature, and wash validation.
- Low Value Content appears here as the purveyor or maker's mark.

### Full back

- Reserved for designs whose title or heraldry adds a second joke.
- Must not merely repeat the front.
- Uses the House, title, reign, and issuing office as evidence of fictional authority.

## Pilot: No. 03 — Doomscrolling

The V2 pilot combines the original circular research-department typography with the existing Duke of Doomscrolling heraldry.

- **Front:** `I’M NOT DOOMSCROLLING. I’M DOING MARKET RESEARCH.`
- **Institution:** Royal Institute of Digital Inquiry
- **Sleeve:** House of Scroll seal
- **Back title:** Duke of Doomscrolling
- **Back issue line:** House of Scroll · Scroll I, the Endless
- **Garment:** washed oxblood heavyweight tee
- **Ink:** warm ivory and muted antique gold, with minimal navy if needed

### V2 to V3 refinement

V2 established the basic merger but remained too decorative. V3 removes the fleur-de-lis devices from the front, replaces the sleeve crest with a direct House-of-Scroll arrow, reduces and simplifies the back arms, and adds a small non-scannable QR-style seal at the upper neck. The joke becomes clearer, and the Realm reads as a designed system rather than a costume theme.

Both studies remain archived in `mockups/realm-merge/`; V3 is the current storefront study.

## Refinement gates before propagation

1. Decide whether the large back crest is essential or whether the title and smaller seal are funnier.
2. Reduce the ornament until the joke still reads as contemporary rather than costume merchandise.
3. Redraw all approved elements as editable vector artwork.
4. Test the front alone, back alone, and complete garment at realistic print size.
5. Sample the ink count and placement with the selected Printify provider.
6. Do not treat generated mockups as print-ready files.

Once the pilot is accepted, apply the same grammar to the remaining original five before revisiting the four fully heraldic issues.
