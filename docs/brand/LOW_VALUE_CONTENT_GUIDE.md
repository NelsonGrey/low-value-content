# Low Value Content Brand and Content Guide

Status: working guide for a pre-launch apparel concept. The governing fiction is in the [Constitution of Engagement](CONSTITUTION_OF_ENGAGEMENT.md); the launch assortment and per-design rules are in the [launch matrix](../product/launch-collection-matrix-v3.md) and the [apparel manifest](../product/apparel-manifest.md), which win where they differ from this guide. It translates the business plan into reusable direction for product, website, social, email, packaging, and customer support. Creative priority is not legal clearance; every name, saying, design, font, and third-party reference must complete the appropriate review before commercial release.

## Brand foundation

Low Value Content makes wearable observations about the systems, rituals, and small humiliations of online life. It is satire from inside the feed: written for people who participate in digital culture while remaining skeptical of what it rewards.

**Brand promise:** Each release should be specific enough to feel recognized, concise enough to read at a glance, and designed well enough to be worn after the joke is understood.

Low Value Content is an editorial apparel brand, not a catalog of disconnected novelty sayings. It combines dry cultural observation, restrained graphic design, and deliberately selected garments. The target is the system and its incentives—not an individual user, a protected group, or a person with less power.

### Audience

- Professionals and managers expected to perform a public career narrative.
- Creators and marketers who depend on systems they also criticize.
- Technology workers who recognize platform mechanics and automated content.
- People who understand doomscrolling, notification anxiety, and the gap between online and offline life.
- Gift buyers looking for a precise observation rather than a generic occupation joke.

## Editorial voice

The voice is dry, exact, self-aware, and implicated. It observes rather than lectures. It rarely explains the joke and never sounds like a brand pretending to be a person on social media.

1. **Make the system visible.** Expose the incentive, interface, ritual, or contradiction beneath ordinary behavior.
2. **Include the speaker.** Admit participation instead of sneering from outside.
3. **Prefer recognition to punchlines.** Aim for “uncomfortably accurate.”
4. **Use restraint.** One clear observation beats layers of slang and decoration.
5. **Keep the shelf life.** Avoid concepts that depend entirely on a temporary meme.

Fitting structures include system messages, corporate qualifications, product language applied to people, quiet confessions, and contradictory status labels.

### Boundaries

- Do not target protected classes, private individuals, or vulnerable people.
- Do not use harassment, threats, slurs, or humiliation as the joke.
- Do not treat addiction, mental illness, disability, or economic insecurity as a disposable punchline.
- Do not use another company’s logos, characters, interface trade dress, or protected slogans without review.
- Do not call a saying unique, exclusive, available, or cleared because a quick search found no exact match.
- Do not use the registered trademark symbol unless registration has issued for the relevant use.

## Visual system

The system should feel like editorial design crossed with institutional interfaces: considered typography, neutral space, simple rules, small labels, restrained diagrams, and occasional high-visibility utility color.

- Favor neutral substantial garments, subject to sampling.
- Prefer one-color artwork where practical.
- Use typography and information design instead of generic clip art.
- Abstract interface references rather than copying a real platform.
- Use a small, consistent source-identifying brand detail on the neck label, packaging, product page, or secondary placement.
- Avoid loud novelty-shirt layouts, unlicensed assets, fake performance metrics, and mockups that imply an unapproved product specification.

### House palette and garments

The Royal House is the visible brand and Low Value Content is the label on it. The same lockup appears everywhere: "The Royal House of Engagement" with "Purveyed by Low Value Content" beneath it. Page titles end "| The Royal House of Engagement". The founding collection is called **the founding collection** (ten issues); do not use "Drop 001", "Founding Ten", "Founding Six" or "Founding Plate" in public copy.

- **Garments:** four colours only: Ivory, Oxblood, Navy, Black. Colours are planned until sampling confirms the blank and provider.
- **Inks (two-ink designs):** navy, oxblood and antique gold. No editorial black, lime or notification red.
- **Shared frame (Nos. 01–06):** every two-ink design carries the House frame (double rule, diamond corners) and the imprint line "The Royal House of Engagement · No. NN". Headline typography may stay in its own joke register (typewriter, auction, report); small text is set in the House serif. Sources are `design/prototypes/01..06`; run `python3 scripts/render-front-art.py` to rebuild the display PNGs and proof sheet.
- **Product pages show shoppers:** name, price, art, one line of description, collection, garment, and an archive note for the lore. Production status, print gates, realm intensity and similar workshop fields live in `docs/`, not on the site.

### Two registers

The visual system has two registers. **Public Notices** use the restrained editorial register above: typography, status language, one or two inks, neutral garments. **Court & Orders** and **Royal Commemorations** use the Realm register: heraldry, seals, Latin as subordinate texture, and the navy, oxblood, antique gold, and ivory palette, with full-colour art for Nos. 07–10. The brand voice stays dry and exact in both; the Realm adds ceremony, not a different joke. Public Notices never receive sovereign heraldry.

The ten collections are Social Media Theater, Algorithm Anxiety, Creator Economy, Chronically Online, Artificial Authenticity, Future of Work, Productivity Theater, Performative Sustainability, Terms and Conditions, and Optimized Human.

The launch assortment is the **Founding Ten**. It begins with the original six creative priorities:

2. Thought leader. No thoughts required.
3. I’m not doomscrolling. I’m doing market research.
4. Content creator. Meaning pending.

These are not evidence of rights clearance or final production approval.

## Product standards

Before release, confirm final production artwork and licensed typography; the exact garment, fiber, weight, construction, colors, print method, dimensions, measurements, model sizing, care instructions, timing, geographic availability, and return rules. Physically approve every offered design and color combination.

The sample test covers print alignment, opacity, edge quality, placement, collar shape, body length, shrinkage, size consistency, cracking, fading, color migration, print feel, packaging, tracking, delivery time, and unintended manufacturer branding. Wash each candidate at least three times according to the proposed customer instructions.

## Release model

1. Complete name, phrase, artwork, font, domain, and handle review.
2. Finalize ten production designs and sample at least two garment or provider configurations.
3. Build ten complete product pages, policies, analytics, and email capture.
4. Complete a real test order from storefront through delivery.
5. Soft launch and resolve quality or fulfillment defects.
6. Run a defined validation period and record the result.
7. Continue, revise, pause, or move proven designs into efficient production.

The business plan’s internal planning signals are 25 to 50 non-network purchases in four weeks, about 2 percent conversion after traffic quality is considered, acquisition cost below $10, contribution after marketing of at least $8 to $10 per shirt, at least two viable designs, defects below 3 percent, and returns or replacements below 5 percent. These are planning thresholds, not achieved results or public promises.

## Website content system

The home page must explain the premise, show the work, establish the quality philosophy, introduce the collections, state the development status, and route visitors to the field guide. Until commerce is enabled, do not use buy-now language, inventory claims, countdowns, ratings, reviews, or artificial scarcity.

Every future product page should contain the saying and collection; accurate front, back, and detail images; the observation behind the design; exact garment and print specifications; fit summary and measurements; colors and sizes; production and shipping timing; care; returns; any QR experience and privacy explanation; and honest availability and price.

The about page should explain why the brand exists, who it is for, what it targets, how products are selected, and the Nelson Grey LLC relationship. Do not invent a founder biography, origin anecdote, manufacturing claim, customer count, or social proof.

## Campaign content

The launch sequence is: the premise, one accurate post for each launch design, product-quality evidence, the field guide, a vote among reviewed next concepts, and a candid result report. A social post should contain one observation, the familiar scene behind it, accurate product imagery, the true status, and one action.

The email sequence is: welcome and expectations; first-drop preview; sample report; launch details; post-purchase care and support; and later editorial drop notes. Discounting should not become the voice.

## QR experience

The front must deliver a complete thought. A future upper-back QR code may reward curiosity with a continuation, reveal, animation, or deliberately low-value result before offering a quiet store path.

- Use a permanent owned-domain URL and printed short URL fallback.
- Keep the destination changeable through a controlled redirect.
- Show the payoff before asking for email or purchase.
- Require no app, account, or unnecessary permission.
- Track only aggregate information needed to compare designs and batches.
- Test the actual print flat, worn, wrinkled, stretched, and washed across common phones.
- Include the second print location in product economics.

## Customer support voice

Support is direct, calm, and less satirical than marketing. Never make the customer the joke. State what happened, the current estimate or policy, what information is needed, and the available next step. For sizing, direct customers to actual garment measurements rather than promising that a labeled size will fit.

## Claims and approval checklist

Before publishing, confirm that rights review is complete; assets and fonts are owned or licensed; the product shown matches the offer; price, stock, specifications, and timing are current; sustainability, origin, quality, and performance claims have evidence; metrics are correctly labeled; testimonials are authentic and permissioned; platform references are reviewed; the call to action works; and previews are explicitly labeled.
