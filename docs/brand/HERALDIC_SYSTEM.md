# Heraldic System of Engagement

Status: working visual canon. This document governs the relationship between the master arms, Great House badges, Orders, lineage devices, standards, apparel, packaging, and website imagery.

## The visual hierarchy

1. **The sovereign arms** represent the Royal House of Engagement as a whole. The peacock, parrot, notification shield, and Wi-Fi crown are reserved for the Crown and its highest warrants.
2. **The Royal Standard** carries the six House charges beneath a central verified device. It represents the Realm in assembly rather than any single House.
3. **Great House badges** identify the six permanent branches of government. They share one frame and differ only in their central charge.
4. **Order insignia** are medals of cross-House distinction. They are more ornamental than a House badge and must remain visibly subordinate to the sovereign arms.
5. **Lineage devices** represent a reign, event, or claimant. They may borrow a House charge when the historical relationship is direct.
6. **Ceremonial materials** include seals, warrants, appointment cards, standards, tissue, boxes, hangtags, and provenance records. They extend the institution without adding a second print location to a launch garment.

## Great House charges

| Great House | Charge | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Prompt | command chevron and waiting cursor | production without prior understanding |
| Scroll | descending feed and arrow | attention without natural conclusion |
| Influence | radiant faceted star | importance established by visibility |
| Signal | measured bars and ascending trace | evidence arranged into a favorable account |
| Spam | repeated envelopes | universal correspondence without invitation |
| Buffer | incomplete loading ring | availability perpetually imminent |

Each badge uses the same crown, circular double rule, botanical supporters, shield, navy field, ivory face, gold line, and oxblood charge. Changes of reign may alter precedence, but must not redesign the Houses.

## Orders

- **Order of the Blue Check:** an eight-point ceremonial star surrounding the verified check. Its blue field is the sole standing exception to the core four-color palette.
- **Order of Jacquard:** a medal bearing a punched card crossed by a continuous woven thread.

## Lineage devices

- Moth the Pious receives the ceremonial moth.
- LO the Interrupted receives the interrupted terminal.
- Y2K the Almost receives the divided double zero.
- Spam the Unsolicited, Scroll the Endless, and Prompt the Confident inherit the corresponding Great House badge.
- Other reigns may use the nearest constitutional charge until a dedicated device is entered into the record.

## Use on the website

- The original identity board is Plate I and must appear as a substantial archival image, not a thumbnail or incidental footer element.
- Apparel and packaging studies belong in Regalia as evidence of the full material system. They are concept studies, not Printify product photography.
- The home page introduces the sovereign arms, Founding Plate, and the six-branch assembly.
- Realm owns the complete House and Order system.
- Lineage owns succession devices.
- Calendar uses the relevant device for each observance.
- Regalia separates garment print art from the ceremonial materials that surround it.

## Apparel rule

The visual institution does not need to appear on every shirt. Public Notices remain primarily typographic. Court, Order, and Royal Commemoration designs may use a badge, medal, seal, supporter, or sovereign device when that element establishes the garment's authority. Every launch garment must still resolve as a complete front-only design.
